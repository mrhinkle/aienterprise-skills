# Worked Examples

All references are described in paraphrase. No dialogue or lyrics are reproduced.

## Context window

**What it is**
A context window is the amount of information an AI model can actively keep in view while it works on a response. The larger the window, the more instructions, documents, and conversation history it can use at once.

**1980s analogy**
Think of the talking car in *Knight Rider*. It can only react to what is on its sensors and dashboard display right now. If something is outside that live view, the driver has to bring it back into range before the car can use it.

**Why it matters in business**
A larger context window helps with long documents, multi-step workflows, and conversations that need continuity. Important details get dropped less often.

**Keep in mind**
A big context window is not long-term memory. It is a larger workbench, not a filing cabinet.

## AI agents

**What it is**
An AI agent takes a goal, decides on steps, uses tools, and acts with limited autonomy. Agents help when work involves several decisions or actions rather than a single answer.

**1980s analogy**
Picture *MacGyver* dropped into an office workflow. You hand over a mission, he looks at whatever tools are in the room, and he works out a sequence of moves to get it done.

**Why it matters in business**
Agents can cut manual coordination across repetitive work: research, follow-ups, document drafting, and system updates.

**Keep in mind**
Real agents need boundaries. An improviser with no supervision is fun on TV and risky in production.

## Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)

**What it is**
RAG lets a model look up relevant documents from your own sources before it answers, then base the answer on what it found. It keeps responses tied to current, company-specific information without retraining the model.

**1980s analogy**
Think of a *Choose Your Own Adventure* book used as a reference manual. Instead of answering from memory, the reader flips to the pages that match the question, reads them, and then responds.

**Why it matters in business**
Answers can cite internal policies, product docs, or contracts, and they update when the documents do.

**Keep in mind**
RAG is only as good as what it retrieves. Bad or stale documents produce confident, wrong answers.

## Guardrails

**What it is**
Guardrails are rules and checks around an AI system that block unsafe, off-topic, or noncompliant inputs and outputs.

**1980s analogy**
The bumpers at a bowling alley's kids' lane. The ball can still go anywhere down the lane, but it cannot drop into the gutter.

**Why it matters in business**
They reduce legal, brand, and data-leak risk while still letting people use the tool freely.

**Keep in mind**
Bumpers do not aim the ball. Guardrails prevent some failures; they do not make the answers good.
