# Research basis

These sources explain why the skill separates message strategy, reader response, evidence, voice, and compliance. They are references, not magic formulas; current law and platform rules should be rechecked before publication.

1. Claude C. Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising* (1923), Library of Congress. Early primary source for specificity, complete salesmanship, and measured test campaigns: https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/
2. Eugene M. Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, Bottom Line Books edition, 2004, ISBN 978-0-88723-298-5. Bibliographic record for the work associated with stages of awareness and market sophistication: https://books.google.com/books/about/Breakthrough_Advertising.html?id=vSmitwAACAAJ
3. Gary Bencivenga, “Marketing Maxim 12.” Primary practitioner source for urgent problem, unique promise, proof, and user-friendly proposition: https://marketingmaxims.com/maxims/twelve
4. Joseph Sugarman, *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*, Wiley. Primary bibliographic source for preparation, sequencing, and the “slippery slide”: https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Adweek+Copywriting+Handbook-p-9780470051245
5. Michael Masterson (Mark Ford) and Mike Palmer, *Copy Logic! The New Science of Producing Breakthrough Copy (Without Criticism)*, AWAI, 2009, ISBN 978-0-9821500-1-6. Publisher page for the CUB critique and four-point checklist: https://www.awai.com/copy-logic/p/
6. Federal Trade Commission, “Advertising and Marketing Basics.” Current U.S. truth-in-advertising entry point: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing
7. Federal Trade Commission, “FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation.” Primary source for reasonable-basis and advertised-level substantiation: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation
8. Federal Trade Commission, *.com Disclosures*. Primary guidance on proximity, prominence, presentation, and actual communication of online disclosures: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/bus41-dot-com-disclosures-information-about-online-advertising.pdf
9. Federal Trade Commission, “The FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking.” Current guidance for honest endorsements and material connections: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
10. Nielsen Norman Group, “The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice” and “The Impact of Tone of Voice on Users' Brand Perception.” Research basis for observable tone coordinates: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/ and https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/
11. Mailchimp, *Content Style Guide*. A public example of separating stable voice from context-sensitive tone and privileging clarity, usefulness, and truth: https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/
12. W3C Web Accessibility Initiative, *Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2*: perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust content, including text alternatives, contrast, reflow, keyboard access, captions, and media alternatives. https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
13. UK Committee of Advertising Practice, *The CAP Code*: non-broadcast advertising rules for misleading advertising, substantiation, comparisons, testimonials, prices, promotions, and category-specific claims. https://www.asa.org.uk/codes-and-rulings/advertising-codes/non-broadcast-code.html
14. UK Information Commissioner's Office, *Direct Marketing Guidance*: privacy and electronic-marketing requirements that may apply alongside advertising rules. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/
15. Federal Trade Commission, “Selling a Work-at-Home or Other Business Opportunity? Revised Rule May Apply to You”: current business guidance for covered offers, written substantiation, earnings-claim statements, purchaser denominators, disclosures, and records. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1
16. Federal Trade Commission, “Back up those earnings claims—and other lessons from the FTC's Labor Task Force work” (2026): current enforcement guidance that unusually successful results do not support claims about what others are likely to earn. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/06/back-those-earnings-claims-other-lessons-ftcs-labor-task-force-work
17. Federal Trade Commission, “The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers”: current guidance on fake or false testimonials, AI-generated stock avatars, virtual influencers, and celebrity-avatar permission. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
18. European Union, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50: transparency duties for certain AI-generated or manipulated content; verify application dates and local implementation for the intended release. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng
19. Federal Trade Commission, *Green Guides*: U.S. guidance on qualification and substantiation of general environmental benefit, carbon offset, certification, recyclable, recycled-content, compostable, degradable, renewable, free-of, and non-toxic claims. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/green-guides

The named classic practitioners disagree on execution and wrote for different markets and eras. The durable synthesis used here is: research the reader and product, select one relevant idea, match awareness, make a concrete offer, earn belief with proportionate proof, preserve forward motion, and test against behavior without sacrificing truth.
