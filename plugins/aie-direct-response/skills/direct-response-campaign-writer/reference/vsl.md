# Video sales letter mode

## Discovery gate

Do not write a publishable VSL blind. In addition to the shared brief, establish runtime range, speaker, delivery style, traffic source, viewing context, visual assets, proof that may be shown, CTA destination, caption/transcript needs, and target platform. If these are missing and the user will not decide them, create a timed outline with explicit assumptions.

## Choose the structure by job

- **Short form:** hook/relevance → one problem or desire → one idea/mechanism → one demonstration or tight proof cluster → product/offer → one action. Use when awareness is high, the ask is low-friction, or one belief shift is sufficient. Product identity and action must arrive without narrative padding; the close must not dominate the teaching.
- **Long form:** hook → stakes → old explanation/alternatives → mechanism → staged proof → product as vehicle → objections/fit → offer/terms → close. Use when several named beliefs must change. Map each belief to proof, add a substantive re-hook at each conceptual transition, and prevent long runs of unsupported claims.
- **Editorial long form (“Agora-style”):** sourced discovery → consequential problem/opportunity → surprising but supportable mechanism → escalating evidence → implications → logically earned product reveal → rigorous offer and close. Use only when the narrative is factual and indispensable to the argument. A delayed reveal fails if it exists mainly to conceal the product; “secret,” enemy, suppression, prediction, and conspiracy frames require direct support.

Record the chosen mode and why the audience, traffic, belief burden, proof, and ask require it. The modes must differ in beat sequence, reveal timing, number of belief shifts, proof cadence, re-hook function, narrative burden, and close density—not merely runtime. Length follows the belief work and evidence, not a universal minute count. Cut any segment that does not advance comprehension, belief, or action.

## Production-ready script format

Use a table with:

| Time | Spoken script | Visual / action | On-screen text | Proof / disclosure | Production note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Estimate duration from the actual word count and stated delivery pace; label the estimate. If pace is unstated, assume a range of 140–160 spoken words per minute and label it as an assumption, not a booking promise. Mark pauses, emphasis, pronunciation, demonstrations, b-roll, graphics, lower thirds, and transitions. Keep on-screen text concise enough to read.

Build an audio-complete argument: a listener should understand essential claims and terms without seeing the screen. When necessary information is visual-only, integrate description into the spoken track or specify an accessible alternative.

## Attention without manipulation

- Open with relevance and a credible curiosity gap; pay it off promptly.
- Re-hook at conceptual transitions with a new question, demonstration, consequence, or proof—not repetitive hype.
- Vary evidence, story, explanation, and visual mode to aid comprehension.
- Keep testimonial identity, material connection, typicality, and limitations attached to the testimonial.
- Match spoken claims, visual claims, captions, landing page, and checkout. The total impression controls.
- Do not use false countdowns, fake live cues, autoplay traps, disguised controls, or urgency that survives its claimed deadline.

## Mode acceptance tests

- **Short:** one belief shift; prompt product/offer reveal; one concentrated proof cluster; minimal close; no unexplained detour.
- **Long:** every named belief change has evidence; product reveal follows the mechanism; re-hooks add information; proof recurs near claims; objections and terms precede the close.
- **Editorial:** discovery and narrative are source-backed and necessary; evidence escalates rather than repeats; delayed reveal is earned; no manufactured enemy or unsupported secret; the offer remains proportionate to the editorial argument.

For every mode, complete a passage-level CUB sweep and a segment-handoff map: viewer question entering, answer supplied, belief earned, and next question opened.

## Deliverables and gates

Unless the user asks for only script copy, provide message brief, timed script, storyboard notes, claim/disclosure ledger, caption-ready transcript, cut-down suggestions, three to five hook/headline alternatives tied to distinct hypotheses, CTA variants, and test hypotheses. Before release, verify runtime, every number and quotation, disclosure duration and modality, pronunciation, mobile legibility, captions, necessary visual description, landing-page continuity, and actual offer behavior.

Production-ready status additionally requires verified caption accuracy, transcript equivalence, keyboard-accessible player and controls, pause/stop controls, motion/flash safety, readable overlays, necessary visual description, and accessible CTA/material terms. For every AI-generated or AI-modified voice, face, presenter, avatar, demonstration, proof visual, or testimonial depiction, also require recorded provenance, permission from any implicated real person for the intended synthetic commercial use, and approved disclosure. If essential content/action or synthetic-media provenance, permission, or disclosure is inaccessible or unverified, label the asset `DRAFT — NOT FOR RELEASE`.
