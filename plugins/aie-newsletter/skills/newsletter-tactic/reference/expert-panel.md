# Tactic — expert-panel critique

After the rubric, read the draft as a panel of newsletter specialists. Each
seat critiques only from its own lens and gives concrete, line-level notes
("move the stat into the first sentence"), not general impressions.

If `editions.tactic.expert_panel` in the config lists seats, use those instead.
A seat can be a role (as below) or a named public expert whose published work
you research with a web search before critiquing. Never invent quotes or claim
a real person said something.

## Default seats

| Seat | Lens |
|---|---|
| Platform operator | How the edition renders and behaves on `publication.platform`; recurring elements; code blocks and links in email clients |
| Growth marketer | Acquisition hooks; whether a reader would forward it; the forward and subscribe CTAs |
| Copy chief | Headline and subtitle craft; where the strongest stat sits; sentence-level cuts |
| Morning-read editor | Conversational tone and density for someone reading on a phone before work |
| Conversion specialist | Whether the reader acts: clarity of the first step, friction, the close |
| Growth analyst | Link placement and count; what readers will click first |
| Deliverability lead | Structure, length, and elements that hurt inbox placement |
| Reader advocate | Voice and reader-first framing; anything that serves the writer instead of the reader |

## Sorting the notes

Group duplicate notes, then sort:

- **MUST FIX** — 5 or more seats raise it
- **SHOULD FIX** — 3 or 4 seats raise it
- **NICE TO HAVE** — 1 or 2 seats raise it

Apply every Must Fix and Should Fix, re-run the word count, and re-score the
rubric. Present Nice to Have items to the user as a short list; don't apply
them unasked.
