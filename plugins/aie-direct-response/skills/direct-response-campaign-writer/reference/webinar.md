# Sales webinar mode

Use a webinar when live teaching, demonstration, interaction, and objection handling create value. Do not stretch a VSL into a webinar merely to delay the offer.

## Three belief shifts

The classic “3 Secrets” structure works best as three necessary belief shifts, not three disconnected tips:

1. **Opportunity or vehicle:** why this route can plausibly produce the desired outcome.
2. **Internal feasibility:** why the right-fit attendee can execute it, including effort and prerequisites.
3. **External feasibility:** how time, tools, support, environment, or other constraints are handled.

Name the belief before and after each section. Teach something useful, demonstrate where possible, and attach proof to the claim. Do not caricature the audience's prior beliefs or imply that mindset alone removes real constraints.

## Run of show

Adapt this shape to the audience and runtime:

1. arrival, accessibility, agenda, promise, who the session is or is not for, recording notice, chat/Q&A capture and reuse disclosure, data-use link, and stated replay window;
2. brief authority and origin story tied to the teaching—not a biography dump;
3. Big Idea and roadmap;
4. three belief-shift teaching blocks, each with concept, example/demonstration, proof, limitation, and takeaway;
5. recap and transparent pivot: explain that an offer follows and why it is relevant;
6. offer: outcome, components, implementation path, support, fit, terms, and risk;
7. stack: show only real deliverables and defensible values. Separate included value from standalone price and do not invent price anchors;
8. conversational close: one action, what happens next, any genuine deadline/capacity when one exists, and clear material conditions;
9. Q&A: answer difficult questions directly and preserve approved answers for iteration;
10. final recap and CTA without an endless close loop.

## Slide and speaker deliverables

Provide:

- run-of-show table with timestamps;
- slide-by-slide map with one job per slide;
- speaker script or detailed notes matched to the user's speaking voice;
- interaction plan for polls, chat, demonstrations, exercises, and Q&A;
- proof and disclosure placement;
- stack and offer slides;
- host/moderator cues, contingency notes, and replay edits;
- recording/capture notice, registration-data handoff, participant-content permission status, and stated-versus-actual replay availability;
- registration, reminder, replay, and follow-up message requirements if requested. If the user wants commercial email drafts, hand these requirements and the approved brief/ledger to the `email-launch-writer` skill; do not draft around its consent, suppression, sender-readiness, identification, unsubscribe, and tracking/privacy gates.

Slides support the spoken argument; they do not duplicate paragraphs. Plan readable type, contrast, captions for live or replay use, and spoken explanation of essential charts or visuals.

For regulated or high-risk topics, create an approved claim and answer bank, prohibited-topic list, moderator escalation/no-answer protocol, disclosure refresh cues, and Q&A log. Review improvised answers before replay, clips, transcript, or follow-up use. Never improvise a projection, recommendation, suitability judgment, medical advice, or unsupported claim.

Do not reuse a participant's name, image, chat, question, or testimonial in a commercial replay or clip without the required notice and permission. Keep the actual replay window and access conditions consistent with what registration and live copy promised.

Production-ready status requires accurate live/replay captions as applicable, accessible registration and materials, keyboard-accessible player and controls, pause/stop behavior, motion/flash safety, spoken access to essential visuals, accessible polls/Q&A/CTA, and a replay accessibility check. For any AI-generated or AI-modified host/participant voice, face, presenter, avatar, demonstration, proof visual, or testimonial depiction, require provenance, permission from any implicated real person for the intended synthetic commercial use, and approved disclosure for live, replay, clips, and transcript. Unknown access to essential content/action or unknown synthetic-media provenance, permission, or disclosure blocks release.

## Measurement and iteration

Track registration source, attendance, qualified engagement, CTA interaction, application or purchase completion, refund/cancellation, and downstream quality. Make on-demand access and follow-up explicit where appropriate. Use Q&A and drop-off data to generate belief-gap hypotheses; do not call the relationship causal without an appropriate design. Do not automate an unproven webinar before learning from live delivery.

Benchmarks are contextual, platform-specific, and often vendor-reported. Compare the webinar primarily with its own qualified historical cohorts.
