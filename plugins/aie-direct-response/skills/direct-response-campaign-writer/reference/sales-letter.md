# Sales letter and sales page mode

## Choose the lead deliberately

- **Direct offer or promise:** for high awareness, clear differentiation, strong proof, or deadline-driven house traffic.
- **Problem–solution:** for a recognized painful problem when the mechanism needs explanation.
- **Story or discovery:** for skeptical or less-aware readers when the story is true, relevant, and quickly creates stakes.
- **Mechanism or secret:** when a genuinely differentiated explanation changes how the reader sees the problem; reveal enough substance early to avoid empty mystery.
- **Contrarian claim or prediction:** only when evidence can carry the burden and the angle will not mislead through omission.

Do not select a lead because it sounds dramatic. State the awareness and proof reason for the choice.

## Argument architecture

Adapt the order rather than forcing every section:

1. headline and deck establish reader relevance, idea, and promise;
2. lead earns the next minute and opens the central argument;
3. problem/desire is made specific without melodrama or invented agitation;
4. old explanations or alternatives are treated fairly;
5. mechanism or new opportunity explains why a different result is possible;
6. proof appears next to the claims it supports;
7. product is introduced as the logical vehicle, not a surprise unrelated pitch;
8. benefits connect features to concrete use and consequence;
9. objections, limitations, and fit are answered honestly;
10. offer terms, bonuses, price, guarantee, and urgency are made explicit;
11. CTA states one action and what happens next;
12. P.S. earns its place by restating the decision, adding genuine evidence, or clarifying a deadline—not introducing a second offer.

## Production format

Deliver annotated copy with section purpose comments kept outside the publishable draft. For a web page, include semantic headline hierarchy, descriptive CTA/link labels, text alternatives for informative proof images, disclosure contrast and placement, keyboard access notes, and 320px mobile/reflow notes. Never put essential price, terms, guarantee, evidence, qualification, or CTA only in an image. For a letter, include salutation, body, close, signature, and optional P.S.

Give headline variants based on meaningfully different hypotheses, such as direct promise versus mechanism, rather than synonym swaps. Preserve one primary CTA even if it recurs.

## Quality gates

- The offer can be explained in one plain paragraph.
- The strongest claim appears with its strongest proof.
- A skimmer can understand reader, result, product, proof, price/commitment, and action.
- The full reader receives a coherent argument with no CUB stoppers.
- Removing the product name would not leave generic category copy.
- All material terms and limitations are visible before action.
- A passage-level CUB sweep marks exact confusing, unbelievable, and boring passages and repairs them before the clean draft.
- A section-handoff map records the reader question entering, answer supplied, belief earned, and next question opened; no unsupported logical jump remains.

For a web sales page, production-ready status requires accessible access to every essential claim, proof item, term, qualification, and action: semantic structure, meaningful links/controls, appropriate text alternatives, readable disclosures, contrast of at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text where WCAG 2.2 AA applies, keyboard-operable actions, and comprehension/reflow at 320 CSS pixels. For a print or plain-text letter, apply the access checks relevant to that delivery channel. Unknown or inaccessible essential content or action blocks release.
