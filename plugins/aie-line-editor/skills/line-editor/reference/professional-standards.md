# Professional basis for the line-edit method

Source review date recorded by the original method: 2026-07-13. Source pages were not refreshed for this packaging pass.

Use this reference to calibrate judgment, explain the skill's scope, or update the method when professional guidance changes. Do not load it for every routine edit after the workflow is familiar.

## Working definition

Treat line editing as stylistic editing plus light copyediting:

- improve clarity, coherence, flow, diction, tone, rhythm, and reader effect at sentence and paragraph level;
- correct local grammar, spelling, punctuation, usage, and consistency;
- preserve intended meaning, authorial voice, point of view, and purposeful fiction-specific departures from formal rules;
- maintain a project style sheet and query choices that require authorial authority.

Keep developmental editing, specialist fact-checking, continuity auditing, and proofreading as distinct stages even when a real project allows some overlap.

## Best-practice synthesis

### Define scope before intervention

Identify the audience, medium, purpose, stage, and requested level of intervention. Do not undo upstream work or perform downstream work invisibly. Escalate when a line-level problem is actually structural, technical, legal, ethical, or canonical.

### Use minimal sufficient intervention

Change only what is required to achieve a clear benefit. Preserve intended meaning. Leave purposeful irregularity alone when it serves voice, cadence, point of view, characterization, or reader effect.

### Edit for reader effect, not rule compliance

Match sentence and paragraph mechanics to the scene's content and mood. Let fast action move differently from reflection. Judge active and passive voice by function. Treat grammar and punctuation conventions as defaults with legitimate fiction exceptions.

### Preserve voice and viewpoint

Maintain the author's style, tone, register, perspective, and narrative distance. Avoid making every sentence equally smooth or every character equally articulate. Query extensive rewording that changes how a character thinks or sounds.

### Improve clarity, coherence, and flow

Resolve ambiguity, repair broken logical connections, refine sentence and paragraph construction, make transitions effective, and prefer precise wording. Cut redundancy, empty phrases, and unnecessary modifiers without stripping useful texture.

### Track fiction-specific consistency

Track viewpoint, tense, character details, names, places, timeline, setting, terminology, dialogue treatment, and recurring stylistic choices. Store house mechanics in a style sheet; store story facts in the canon ledger.

### Use queries as editorial tools

Query when the text supports multiple meanings, a correction needs unsupported content, or a decision belongs to the author or a specialist. Make queries concise, respectful, actionable, and easy to answer. Suggest a safe default when possible.

### Apply conscious language contextually

Flag language that may cause unintended harm, but examine character, viewpoint, historical setting, power, and narrative purpose before changing it. Do not erase purposeful discomfort or sanitize a character arc without understanding its function.

### Apply plain language selectively

Use familiar, concrete, audience-appropriate words; reduce jargon; name actors and actions when useful; and resolve ambiguity in technical or governance explanation. Do not convert fiction into uniformly short sentences or optimize literary prose to a reading-age score.

### Verify AI-assisted edits

Reread the revision, inspect the diff, and check all changed facts, names, numbers, quotations, and technical claims. Watch for voice flattening, invented specificity, generic polish, repeated rhetorical patterns, and new errors. Treat automated diagnostics as prompts for judgment, not automatic edits.

## Primary and professional sources

### Editors Canada — Professional Editorial Standards 2024

- [Fundamentals of Editing](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/fundamentals-editing/): scope, collaboration, house style, minimal intervention, preservation of intended meaning, and conscious-language responsibilities.
- [Standards for Stylistic Editing](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/stylistic-editing/): clarity, coherence, flow, language, voice, point of view, narrative effect, mood, rhythm, and selective plain-language practice.
- [Professional Editorial Standards 2024 PDF](https://editors.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/EditorsCanada_ProfessionalEditorialStandards_2024.pdf): copyediting correctness, legitimate fiction exceptions, style sheets, consistency, accuracy checks, and stage boundaries.

### Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading

- [CIEP Competency Framework, August 2025](https://www.ciep.uk/static/f5e4f1ac-8d70-464a-a8debd6e7841a97e/CIEP-Competency-Framework-August-2025.pdf): editorial judgment, respect for authorial voice, ambiguity handling, audience and context, and conscious-language competence.
- [Introduction to Fiction Editing](https://www.ciep.uk/learn-and-develop/training-courses/introduction-to-fiction-editing-e-learning.html): fiction-specific attention to plot, voice, point of view, dialogue, and narrative consistency, including the question of which rules fiction may bend.
- [Creating style sheets for fiction](https://www.ciep.uk/resource/fiction-style-sheets.html): word lists plus tracking of tense, point of view, characters, descriptions, places, timeline, punctuation, and typography.
- [Are you writing for humans? Then work with human editors](https://www.ciep.uk/resource/are-you-writing-for-humans-then-work-with-human-editors.html): the importance of leaving intentional choices alone and thoroughly checking AI-generated edits for harm and voice damage.

### University of Chicago Press

- [The Chicago Manual of Style, 18th edition preface](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/book/ed18/frontmatter/pref.html): current Chicago coverage of fiction, dialogue, punctuation, multilingual narratives, and independent authors.
- [The Chicago Guide to Copyediting Fiction](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo183690033.html), Amy J. Schneider (2023): fiction-copyediting workflow, style sheets, characters, places, timeline, dialogue, grammar, conscious language, and collisions between fact and fiction.
- [Chicago style-sheet tool](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/help-tools/my-manual/style-sheets.html): project-level recording and reuse of editorial decisions.

### Plain-language research

- [GOV.UK content principles and research background](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background): audience-aware vocabulary, concrete and specific wording, active verbs when they suit the purpose, reduced jargon, and the distinction between clarity principles and house voice.

## Reconciliation rules

When sources or defaults conflict:

1. Follow the author's explicit decision.
2. Follow the current manuscript's established and intentional practice.
3. Protect clarity, meaning, voice, and reader effect.
4. Follow the project style sheet.
5. Preserve the manuscript's English variety; apply Chicago 18 as a fallback for U.S. book prose when no project guide is specified.
6. Query rather than force a choice when the conflict is materially authorial.
