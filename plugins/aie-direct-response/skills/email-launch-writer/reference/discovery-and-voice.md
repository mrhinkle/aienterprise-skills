# Launch discovery and voice

## Minimum brief

- Who is the one reader, and how are customers, prospects, waitlist members, affiliates, and unengaged subscribers different?
- How did each segment join, what permission exists, and which jurisdictions apply?
- What do recipients already know from ads, content, webinar, sales page, or prior emails?
- What is the Big Idea, promise, mechanism, offer, price, payment plan, guarantee, renewal/cancellation treatment, real deadline, and one action?
- What proof exists for every important claim? Identify source, date, denominator, typicality, and material connection.
- What are the top objections, disqualifiers, limitations, and support questions?
- What send infrastructure, domain, expected volume, historical complaint rate, and list hygiene constraints exist?
- Which approved samples best represent the sender and this relationship?

For sequences, capture what comparable claims and mechanisms this audience has already heard. Classify the market as early, crowded, or highly skeptical with evidence; higher sophistication raises the differentiation and proof burden rather than the hype level.

## Voice model

Use 3–10 representative samples where possible. Extract:

- reader relationship and level of intimacy;
- serious–funny, formal–casual, respectful–irreverent, and matter-of-fact–enthusiastic coordinates;
- typical subject style, opening, sentence rhythm, paragraph length, punctuation, sign-off, and P.S. behavior;
- recurring vocabulary, stories, humor, metaphors, signature phrases, and forbidden language;
- how the sender makes claims, admits uncertainty, discusses price/risk, and asks for action.

Define `always`, `usually`, `sometimes`, and `never`. Separate stable voice from launch-phase tone: announcement can be expansive, proof precise, objection email empathetic, and deadline email direct while still sounding like one sender.

Do not fabricate intimacy, pretend a broadcast is a personal reply, or imitate a living public figure. Translate influences into high-level traits.

Run an authenticity red-team against approved samples and facts: authority boundaries, worldview, stories, emotional intensity, intimacy, manufactured enemies, borrowed status, and “secret/system/freedom” posture. When unsupported, use plain provisional language.

## Canonical evidence ledger

Use the same schema across email, campaign-writing, and copy-chief handoffs:

| Claim | Type | Source / evidence owner | Status | Support ceiling | Safe wording | Date / population / denominator | Typicality | Testimonial-use fields | Synthetic-media status / subject permission / disclosure | Disclosure | Approval owner | Release blocker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Status is `verified`, `qualified`, `unverified`, `contradicted`, or `not applicable` only when an opinion, aspiration, or obvious puffery creates no objective implication in context. A testimonial-use cell records identity/authenticity, original artifact, exact versus edited/translated wording, date/currentness, permission/release and channel/term, incentive/material connection, typicality, and approved disclosure. The synthetic-media cell records each real-capture, AI-generated, or AI-modified face, voice, avatar, demonstration, proof image, or testimonial depiction; any implicated real person; subject authorization for synthetic commercial use and channel/term; and approved disclosure. Unknown provenance, permission, or disclosure blocks release.
