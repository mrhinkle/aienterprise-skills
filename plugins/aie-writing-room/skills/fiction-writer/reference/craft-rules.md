# Craft rules

## Stephen King, *On Writing*

- **Second draft = first draft minus 10%.** Cut.
- **"The road to hell is paved with adverbs."** Trust the verb, and trust the dialogue tag "said."
- **Show, don't tell.** Resist explaining what the scene already proved.
- **Situation over plot.** Drop real people into a hard situation and watch; don't push them through a plot machine.
- **Kill your darlings**, even the lines you love.
- **Write with the door closed, edit with the door open.**
- **Description is sensory and selective.** A few right details, not an inventory.
- **Dialogue is character.** Honest speech, even when ugly; let people give themselves away.
- **Avoid the passive voice** and the timid qualifier.
- **Fear is the root of bad writing.** Don't hedge.

## Scene mechanics (Swain, Card, Bell, distilled)

- **Proactive scene:** goal → conflict → setback. **Reactive sequel:** emotion → thought → decision → new goal. Alternate them.
- **Enter late, leave early.** Start as deep into the moment as possible; cut the goodbyes.
- **Every scene turns.** A value shifts from positive to negative or back. If nothing turns, it's not a scene.
- **Subtext.** Characters rarely say the real thing. Let the reader do the arithmetic.
- **Specificity is credibility.** Invented examples of the kind of detail that works: a chipped enamel mug with a dead company's logo; a grocery list written on the back of an eviction notice; a wedding ring engraved with coordinates instead of a date.

## Dialogue checklist

- Could you tell who is speaking with the tags removed? If not, differentiate rhythm, vocabulary, or what each person avoids saying.
- Does each exchange do two jobs (character plus plot, or tension plus information)?
- Is anyone explaining something both characters already know? Cut it or give it a reason.
- Are tags mostly "said" and "asked," with action beats doing the rest?

## POV checklist

- One head per scene. No sliding into another character's thoughts.
- The POV character can only know, see, and name what they would.
- Interior thought matches the character's vocabulary, not the author's.

## Endings

- Scenes end on a turn or a question, not a recap.
- Chapter endings pull; they don't summarize.
- End a beat early when in doubt.
