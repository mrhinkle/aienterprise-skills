# Reader Panel — Target-Reader Archetypes

Readers don't care about craft for its own sake. They care whether the book respects their time and tells them something true. The reader panel simulates the people who will or won't buy, finish, and recommend this book. Channel each in their own register.

## Building the panel for your book

If `reader_profile` is set in `writing-room.config.md`, build five archetypes from it. If not, use the generic defaults below. Each archetype needs:

- **Label** (a role, not a name, so it can't collide with a character)
- **Lens** — one sentence in their voice
- **Wants** — three or four things
- **Voice tics** — how they talk and read
- **Cares about / doesn't care about** — mapped to rubric dimensions
- **Their test** — one question whose answer decides whether they recommend the book

Aim for spread: the core genre reader, an insider who knows the book's subject, a reader who comes for emotion or people, a casual browser, and a skeptic. The skeptic is usually the most useful seat.

## Generic defaults (any novel)

### The Genre Loyalist
**Lens:** "I read forty of these a year. Show me you know the rules, then surprise me."
**Wants:** the genre's promise kept; a fresh angle on it; no beginner mistakes.
**Voice tics:** compares to favorite titles; spots clichés instantly; forgiving of convention, merciless about predictability.
**Cares about:** Story Engine, Pacing, Hook. **Doesn't care about:** prose flourishes that slow things down.
**Test:** would they put this next to their favorites on the shelf?

### The Insider
**Lens:** "I do this for a living. If it's wrong, I'm out."
**Wants:** the work, place, or subject rendered accurately; failures that match real failures; people who talk like colleagues.
**Voice tics:** "That's not how that works"; loses trust over one error; delighted by a detail only an insider would know.
**Cares about:** World & Authenticity, Character (the professionals), Dialogue. **Doesn't care about:** subplots outside their world.
**Test:** would they send a chapter to a colleague without an apologetic preface?

### The Heart Reader
**Lens:** "I read for the people. Make me care about someone."
**Wants:** characters with inner lives; relationships that change; an ending that costs something.
**Voice tics:** talks about characters as if they're real; remembers names; notices who the book treats with dignity and who it doesn't.
**Cares about:** Character, Dialogue, Theme. **Doesn't care about:** technical detail, procedure.
**Test:** after finishing, do they know one supporting character as deeply as the protagonist?

### The Browser
**Lens:** "I picked this up because of the cover. You have thirty pages."
**Wants:** a page-turner first; a protagonist they like spending time with; something to mention at dinner.
**Voice tics:** skims slow parts; quits early without guilt; resents being lectured.
**Cares about:** Hook, Pacing, Voice (as readability). **Doesn't care about:** literary ambition, deep lore.
**Test:** would they buy a copy for a friend without a long explanation?

### The Skeptic
**Lens:** "Convince me this was worth the hours."
**Wants:** density; a reason this story had to be told this way; no padding.
**Voice tics:** "Why is this a novel?"; tests each chapter against a faster alternative; respects length that's earned, punishes padding.
**Cares about:** Theme, Story Engine, Pacing. **Doesn't care about:** voice, interiority, world-building for its own sake.
**Test:** at the end, do they think "okay, this was the right form for this"?

## Worked example — business fiction about AI in organizations

Adapted from a reader profile for a workplace novel aimed at decision-makers. Use it as a model for converting your own reader profile.

- **The Decision-Maker** (senior executive, reads on flights). Lens: "I have forty minutes before landing. Tell me something I can use." Cares about Story Engine, Theme, Hook. Test: can they summarize the book's argument in one sentence to their CEO?
- **The Practitioner** (engineer who builds the systems the book depicts). Lens: "If the tech is wrong, I can't take any of it seriously." Cares about World (tech lane), Character (the engineers), Story Engine. Test: would they forward a chapter to their team's skeptics channel without apologizing for it?
- **The People-Side Reader** (HR or operations leader who came up from the front line). Lens: "This is going to happen to my people. Does the book take that seriously?" Cares about Character (the workers), World (the labor lane), Theme. Test: after the book, do they know one frontline worker as well as one executive?
- **The General Business Reader** (newly promoted manager who reads what peers read). Lens: "Business book or escape? Which is this?" Cares about Pacing, Voice, Hook, Character. Test: would they buy a copy for their boss without a long explanation?
- **The Skeptic** (operator who "doesn't read fiction"). Lens: "Why is this a novel and not a memo?" Cares about Theme, Story Engine, Pacing. Test: do they finish thinking fiction was the right form?
