# Research basis

## Copy and sequencing

1. Claude C. Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising* (1923), Library of Congress: specificity, salesmanship, and measured test campaigns. https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/
2. Eugene M. Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, Bottom Line Books edition, 2004, ISBN 978-0-88723-298-5: awareness and sophistication as inputs to message choice. Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Breakthrough_Advertising.html?id=vSmitwAACAAJ
3. Gary Bencivenga, “Marketing Maxim 12”: urgent problem, unique promise, proof, and user-friendly proposition. https://marketingmaxims.com/maxims/twelve
4. Joseph Sugarman, *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*, Wiley: research, conversational continuity, and curiosity. https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Adweek+Copywriting+Handbook-p-9780470051245
5. Frank Kern, official site and *Convert 2.0*: campaign as a sales process and conversational execution. https://www.frankkern.com/
6. Mailchimp, *Content Style Guide*: a public model for clear, useful, human, context-appropriate voice. https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/
7. Nielsen Norman Group, “The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice”: evidence-informed voice calibration. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/

The five-email “Bank Refill” sequence in this skill is an operational synthesis, not a scientific law. Public searches did not establish a single authoritative source for that exact name. Deadline concentration claims vary by marketer, list, offer, attribution, and window, so the skill requires cohort evidence rather than repeating a universal percentage.

## Law, platform rules, and measurement

8. Federal Trade Commission, “CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business”: commercial-message classification, accurate headers and subjects, address, opt-out, and sender responsibility. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
9. Federal Trade Commission, “Advertising and Marketing Basics”: truthful, non-deceptive, evidence-based claims. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing
10. Federal Trade Commission, “Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking”: honest endorsements and clear material-connection disclosure. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
11. Google, “Email sender guidelines”: authentication, DNS/TLS, spam-rate, format, alignment, unsubscribe, and sending-practice requirements for Gmail. https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126
12. Yahoo, “Sender Best Practices”: current requirements and recommendations for authentication, complaints, list hygiene, and unsubscribe. https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/
13. Apple, “Mail Privacy Protection & Privacy”: remote content may load in the background, limiting open tracking. https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/
14. Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission, CASL guidance: consent, identification, and unsubscribe requirements for commercial electronic messages. https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm
15. UK Information Commissioner's Office, “Guidance on direct marketing using electronic mail”: PECR, data protection, consent, soft opt-in, and electronic marketing. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/
16. W3C, *Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2*: text alternatives, contrast, reflow, semantics, and keyboard access. https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/
17. Federal Trade Commission, *Health Products Compliance Guidance*: substantiation for health and safety claims. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance
18. U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, “Investment Adviser Marketing”: anti-misleading, performance, testimonial, endorsement, and recordkeeping considerations. https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-adviser-marketing
19. Federal Trade Commission, “Selling a Work-at-Home or Other Business Opportunity? Revised Rule May Apply to You”: current business guidance for covered offers, written substantiation, earnings-claim statements, purchaser denominators, disclosures, and records. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1
20. Federal Trade Commission, “Back up those earnings claims—and other lessons from the FTC's Labor Task Force work” (2026): current enforcement guidance that unusually successful results do not support claims about what others are likely to earn. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/06/back-those-earnings-claims-other-lessons-ftcs-labor-task-force-work
21. Federal Trade Commission, “The Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: Questions and Answers”: current guidance on fake or false testimonials, AI-generated stock avatars, virtual influencers, and celebrity-avatar permission. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
22. European Union, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 50: transparency duties for certain AI-generated or manipulated content; verify application dates and local implementation for the intended release. https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng
23. Federal Trade Commission, *Green Guides*: U.S. guidance on qualification and substantiation of general environmental benefit, carbon offset, certification, recyclable, recycled-content, compostable, degradable, renewable, free-of, and non-toxic claims. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/green-guides

Platform rules and laws change. Verify the current source, target jurisdiction, message type, and sending volume before launch.
