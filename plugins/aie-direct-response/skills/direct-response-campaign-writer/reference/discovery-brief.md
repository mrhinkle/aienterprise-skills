# Discovery and message brief

## Minimum questions

Group only unanswered questions and explain why any blocker matters.

### Market

- Who is the one reader in the buying situation? What do they already know, believe, want, fear, and distrust?
- What sent them here: cold ad, referral, search, house list, event, affiliate, or existing customer relationship?
- What alternatives—including doing nothing—do they compare?
- What phrases come from real interviews, reviews, support tickets, sales calls, or surveys? Identify source and date.
- Is the market problem or desire urgent and reachable enough to support the offer? Do not expect clever copy to rescue weak demand, poor fit, or an undifferentiated offer.

### Product and offer

- What is sold, how does it work, for whom is it not suitable, and what must the buyer do to get the result?
- What are the exact deliverables, price, payment schedule, tax/shipping treatment, trial, renewal, cancellation, guarantee, bonuses, eligibility, capacity, and deadline?
- Which limitation or tradeoff must a buyer understand before acting?
- What one action should follow the asset?

### Proof

- What product records, demonstrations, customer data, studies, credentials, testimonials, comparisons, and operational facts are available?
- Which results are typical, which are exceptional, and what denominator and timeframe apply?
- What claims are prohibited, preapproved, or subject to legal/compliance review?
- For each testimonial, is there an authentic source artifact, exact approved wording, current experience, identity verification, release/permission for this channel and term, incentive or material connection, typicality support, and approved disclosure?

### Voice and production

- Supply 3–10 representative writing or speaking samples when possible.
- Name stable voice traits, forbidden phrases, pronunciation, reading level, and channel constraints.
- For video or webinars, identify speaker, delivery pace, runtime, visual resources, accessibility requirements, and platform.
- Identify target countries, states/provinces, regulated categories, and approval owners.

## Awareness and sophistication

Classify current awareness as unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or most-aware. Treat these as a planning model, not a diagnosis. State evidence for the classification.

Estimate market sophistication:

- early: a direct credible promise may be novel;
- crowded: explain a differentiated mechanism or proof;
- highly skeptical: narrow claims, show method and limitations, and let proof carry the argument.

Do not increase hype merely because a market is crowded.

## Voice profile

Extract relationship to reader; serious–funny, formal–casual, respectful–irreverent, and matter-of-fact–enthusiastic coordinates; sentence rhythm; vocabulary; metaphor and humor patterns; signature and forbidden language; and how the voice discusses uncertainty and risk.

Separate stable voice from situational tone. Express the usable profile as `always`, `usually`, `sometimes`, and `never` behaviors. If samples conflict, prefer the same speaker, channel, audience, and most recent approved material. If none exists, use a neutral, clear voice and label it provisional.

Do not imitate a named living writer. Convert named influences into high-level techniques and write in the user's own voice.
