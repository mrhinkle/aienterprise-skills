# Tactic — structure

Short by design. The default range is 350–750 words, and the max is a hard cap
(`editions.tactic.words`).

## Skeleton

````markdown
# [Title]

*[Italic subtitle naming the deliverable — e.g., "The 75-word agent template
that fact-checks a draft before it ships"]*

[One intro paragraph, first person. The problem the reader has, the tactic that
solves it, and the reframe: what the reader becomes once they use it. 3–6
sentences. No warm-up.]

[Featured image]

[One line introducing the artifact, then the artifact:]

```
[The copy-paste artifact: a template, prompt, or short config, with
[BRACKETED] fill-ins so the reader can adapt it. Plain code block, no
language tag.]
```

**Sample output** ([one line of context]):

```
[A short, realistic sample of what the artifact produces when run]
```

**What changes:** [What the reader stops doing themselves and what they do
instead. The payoff in one or two sentences.]

**Where else this works:** [3–4 adjacent uses. Same skeleton, different
specifics. One sentence.]

[Short closing paragraph: the news hook. What shipped, when, and its
availability, cited to the source. Close on a nudge to act this week.]
````

## Section notes

- **Title and subtitle** — the subtitle names the thing the reader gets.
- **Intro** — one paragraph. If it needs two, the tactic is too big.
- **Artifact** — complete, bracketed fill-ins, works as pasted.
- **Sample output** — realistic, short, clearly labeled as a sample.
- **What changes / Where else this works** — bolded callouts, one or two
  sentences each.
- **Close** — the news hook with a citation, then the nudge.
