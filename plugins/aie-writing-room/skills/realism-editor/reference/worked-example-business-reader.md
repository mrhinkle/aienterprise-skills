# Worked Example — Reader Profile for Business Fiction About AI

A filled-in reader profile for a business novel (in the vein of workplace fables and technothrillers) about organizations adopting AI. Adapt it, or use it as a model for your own reader.

## Who this is for
Business professionals figuring out AI inside real organizations: CIOs and CISOs, operations and supply-chain leaders, mid-market CEOs and boards, and the practitioners who report to them. They are not researchers. They are the people who have to decide and then answer for it.

## What they already know
How budgets, boards, vendors, and procurement work. Roughly what a language model is. They don't need contracts explained; they do need model internals turned into consequences.

## The real dilemmas they live
- **Build vs. buy vs. rent:** own the models or depend on a platform that gets smarter on your data and locks you in.
- **Data control and lock-in:** "If our data goes through their model, do we still have an edge?"
- **Speed vs. safety and oversight:** the delay a review step adds versus the failure it prevents.
- **Return and survival:** AI as a way into work you couldn't previously bid on, not only cost-cutting, under margin and board pressure.
- **Workforce:** displacement, reskilling, who keeps their job, and whether the new roles are good ones.
- **Trust, bias, liability, regulation:** the model that is confident and wrong; who is liable when it fails; the rule that could outlaw your approach.

## What lands
- Concrete mechanics: contracts, RFPs, procurement clocks, board votes, loan covenants, insurance, payroll.
- Trade-offs with a price. Nobody wins for free.
- Workers rendered without sentimentality or contempt.
- A skeptic who is half right (on liability, on cost) rather than a villain who is all wrong.

## What to cut
- Techno-utopia (AI fixes everything) and doomer cliché (AI ends everything).
- Jargon without stakes; demos that work flawlessly; regulators and competitors as cartoons.
- Solutions that cost nothing. Privacy-preserving infrastructure, oversight, and retraining are expensive and slow; say so.

## Realism bar
Thriller compression of time is fine. Business mechanics, approvals, and costs must be ones a real operator would sign.

## Gut-check line
"How does this help a business professional think more clearly about using AI, today?"
