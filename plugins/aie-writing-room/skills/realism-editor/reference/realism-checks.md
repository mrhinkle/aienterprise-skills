# Realism Checks

The recurring ways a scene stops feeling real to the reader it was written for. Each has a quick test and a typical fix.

## Framing failures

| Failure | Test | Typical fix |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Utopia | Does the new thing (tech, policy, leader) fix everything? | Give it a real cost, a side effect, or a group it fails |
| Doom | Does it end everything with no one able to act? | Show the ordinary levers people actually pull |
| Jargon without stakes | Could you delete the term and lose nothing? | Replace with what it costs or who it hurts |
| Flawless demo | Does something work perfectly the first time on the page? | Add the glitch, the workaround, the person who stays late |

## People failures

| Failure | Test | Typical fix |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Strawman skeptic | Is the doubter wrong about everything? | Make them right about one real thing (cost, liability, timing) |
| Cartoon institution | Do regulators, competitors, HR, or the board act with no internal logic? | Give them an incentive and a constraint of their own |
| Saint or prop worker | Are working people noble victims or comic relief? | Give them strategy, humor, and something to lose |
| Hero exec | Does the leader decide without friction from money, board, or law? | Put a covenant, a vote, a deadline, or a lawyer in the room |

## Mechanics failures

| Failure | Test | Typical fix |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Free win | Did anyone get the outcome without paying for it? | Name the price: money, time, a relationship, a job |
| Vanishing cost | Was a cost set up and then forgotten a chapter later? | Carry it forward or pay it off on the page |
| Wrong clock | Does a process move at plot speed rather than real speed (procurement, hiring, courts, hospitals)? | Use the real clock, or have a character fight it |
| Missing paperwork | Is there no contract, approval, form, or record where the reader knows one exists? | Add the one document that matters |
| Wrong vocabulary | Does a character describe their own job in outsider words? | Use the verbs of the actual work |

## Too-clean endings

- A villain whose defeat costs the heroes nothing.
- A victory that solves the structural problem, not just this instance.
- A reconciliation with no residue.

The fix is rarely to make things grim. It is to leave a real bill on the table.

## The gut-check line

Adapt this to your reader profile: "How does this scene help [the intended reader] see [their real situation] more clearly?" If it doesn't, ground it until it does.
