# Voice and evidence protocol

## Build a usable voice profile

Prefer 3–10 representative samples from the same brand, speaker, audience, and channel. Do not infer a brand voice from one anomalous sentence when better evidence exists.

Extract observable patterns:

- relationship to reader: peer, guide, expert, challenger, host;
- tone coordinates: serious–funny, formal–casual, respectful–irreverent, matter-of-fact–enthusiastic;
- sentence length, rhythm, fragments, paragraph density, punctuation, and contractions;
- vocabulary, metaphors, humor, taboo phrases, signature phrases, and preferred calls to action;
- stance: how directly the voice promises, challenges, admits uncertainty, and discusses risk;
- channel shifts: what stays constant and what changes in email, page, spoken script, or live delivery.

Return a compact profile with `always`, `usually`, `sometimes`, and `never` behaviors. Separate **voice** (stable identity) from **tone** (situational adjustment). If no samples exist, ask for them or use a neutral conversion voice and label it as provisional.

Do not imitate a named living writer or public figure. Translate a requested influence into high-level traits and techniques, then write in the user's own voice.

Run an authenticity red-team: compare claimed authority, worldview, selling posture, emotional intensity, level of intimacy, stories, enemies, status cues, and “secret/system/freedom” tropes with approved samples and factual records. Do not introduce a pseudo-confession, struggle-to-success story, manufactured enemy, borrowed authority, or private familiarity merely because it is common in direct response.

## Evidence ledger

Create an atomic ledger for material claims using this canonical schema so it can pass unchanged between the writing and review skills:

| Claim | Type | Source / evidence owner | Status | Support ceiling | Safe wording | Date / population / denominator | Typicality | Testimonial-use fields | Synthetic-media status / subject permission / disclosure | Disclosure | Approval owner | Release blocker |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Types include product fact, performance/result, quantitative comparison, expert claim, customer experience, credential, scarcity/deadline, price/value, guarantee, or research claim.

Statuses:

- `verified` — the supplied source directly supports the wording and scope;
- `qualified` — support exists but requires narrower wording or a nearby limitation;
- `unverified` — no adequate support supplied;
- `contradicted` — available evidence conflicts with the claim;
- `not applicable` — opinion, aspiration, or obvious puffery that creates no objective implication in context.

Check evidence quality, date, population, denominator, comparison baseline, causality, typicality, and source independence. A testimonial proves that a person reported an experience; it does not automatically prove typical results or causation.

For each testimonial or endorsement also record identity/authenticity, original source artifact, exact versus edited or translated wording, date and whether the experience is current, permission/release status, permitted channels and term, incentive or material connection, typicality support, and approved disclosure. Any unresolved authenticity, quote-integrity, permission, or disclosure field is Blocking for release; retain a visible placeholder rather than a clean quotation.

For every face, voice, presenter, avatar, demonstration, proof image, or testimonial depiction, record whether it is real capture, AI-generated, or AI-modified; whose identity or likeness is implicated; subject authorization for synthetic commercial use and permitted channels/term; and the required approved disclosure. A synthetic depiction must not imply that a nonexistent person is a customer, that a real person said or did something they did not, or that a simulated demonstration is real proof. Unknown provenance, permission, or disclosure is Blocking.

When support is missing, recommend one of four repairs: provide evidence, narrow the claim, convert it to an explicitly framed opinion or aspiration when honest, or remove it.
