# Measurement and experiment design

## Metrics

Choose the metric closest to the business and reader outcome:

- primary: qualified application, completed purchase, contribution margin, or another defined conversion;
- diagnostic: delivered rate, qualified click, landing-page progression, checkout start, reply, or support question;
- guardrails: unsubscribe, complaint, bounce, refund/cancellation, chargeback, low-quality application, and downstream activation or retention.

Treat opens as directional at best. Apple Mail Privacy Protection can load remote content without user engagement and prevents senders from reliably learning whether a recipient opened a message. Do not use opens alone to declare a winner or trigger sensitive personalization.

## Test hierarchy

Prefer hypotheses with material learning value:

1. audience/segment and eligibility;
2. offer, price presentation, or risk reversal when authorized;
3. Big Idea, promise, or mechanism;
4. proof type and objection sequence;
5. CTA and destination continuity;
6. subject/preheader and presentation.

Test one interpretable difference at a time when causal learning matters. Predefine eligible population, randomization unit and assignment, allocation, primary metric, minimum detectable effect or practical threshold, duration, sample-size/power logic, locked attribution window and model, exclusions, guardrails, and decision rule. For multiple variants, metrics, segments, or interim looks, predeclare the family of comparisons and multiplicity or sequential-testing control; otherwise label results exploratory. Before send, QA event firing, identity joins, bot/automation filtering, duplicate conversion handling, missing data, and treatment leakage. Check sample-ratio mismatch before interpreting results. Avoid peeking until significance, calling tiny samples winners, or generalizing one list's result to all lists.

## Campaign reporting

Report by send, segment, acquisition source, device where reliable, and cohort. Include:

- eligible, attempted, delivered, bounced, and suppressed counts;
- qualified clicks and conversion events;
- orders/applications and net revenue or quality outcome;
- unsubscribe, complaint, refund, and cancellation signals;
- attribution window and model;
- offer/deadline incidents;
- lessons and the next test.

Do not say “60% of sales came from the last two emails” unless the actual campaign data, attribution method, denominator, and window support that statement. Even then, describe it as a finding from that cohort, not a universal rule.
