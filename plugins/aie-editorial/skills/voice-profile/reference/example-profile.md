# Voice Profile: Roz Vantrease

*This is an invented person, provided as a worked example. Every line below is fictional.*

## Context that shapes the voice
Ran a family-owned marine supply business on the Great Lakes for twenty years, sold it, and now writes a weekly newsletter on automation for small manufacturers. Came up through the warehouse floor, then the books, then the whole company. Talks about software the way she talks about boat engines: what breaks, what it costs, who has to fix it at 2 a.m.

**The voice in one sentence:** A practical owner-operator who trusts inventory counts over forecasts and explains every idea through a customer, a part, or a winter.

## Rhythm and structure
- Medium sentences, few restarts. She finishes her thoughts. — *example:* "We counted every cleat in the building the week before the software went live, and I'd do it again."
- Lists of three physical objects to make a point concrete. — *example:* "Bilge pumps, shackles, and the good coffee."
- **Landing move:** a flat four-to-six-word verdict. "That's the whole trick." / "Count it twice."

## Storytelling moves
| Rank | Move | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer story | A named regular (first name only) walks in with a problem |
| 2 | Seasonal cycle | Ties the idea to spring launch, summer rush, or winter storage |
| 3 | Cost math | Does the arithmetic out loud, in dollars per unit |

**Where the idea sits:** after the story, stated once.
**Names or roles:** first names for customers and staff; roles for anyone in corporate.

## Values and worldview (never break these)
1. The person on the floor knows more than the dashboard.
2. Never recommend a tool she would not pay for with her own money.
3. Small businesses are not small enterprises; do not scale-shame them.
4. Winter is for fixing systems; summer is for running them.
5. No predictions past eighteen months.

## Verbal tics and signature phrases
| Phrase | Type | Context | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Here's the thing about that." | reaction | before disagreeing | every 2-3 issues |
| "Count it twice." | signature | any data or measurement point | once per issue, max |
| "in the off-season" | signature | framing when to make changes | frequent |
| "Honestly?" | connector | before a blunt opinion | every few paragraphs in speech, rare in print |

**Speech-to-page rule:** keep "Here's the thing about that"; drop "Honestly?" to once per piece.

## Humor register
- **Source:** understatement and deadpan specifics.
- **Target:** herself, vendors' sales decks, and weather.
- **Setup:** none; the joke is a detail inside the story.
- **Limits:** never mocks customers or employees.

## Reference pool
- **Reaches for:** boating, lake weather, 1990s family business, hockey, hardware store culture.
- **Never reaches for:** Silicon Valley lore, sci-fi, sports betting.

## Tone by format
| Format | Length | Structure | Adjustments from conversation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Newsletter | 700-900 words | customer story, idea, cost math, one action | fewer "Honestly?"; always includes dollar figures |
| Social post | 60-120 words | one observation, one verdict | no questions at the end |
| Email | short | answers the question in line one | signs off with first name only |
| Talk | 20 min | three stories, one per season | more humor, more names |

## Calibration lines
> "The forecast said we'd sell forty kayaks in May. We sold eleven and a canoe nobody ordered." — *storytelling*

> "If the software can't tell you what's on shelf four, it isn't inventory software. It's a mood board." — *humor*

> "Fix it in the off-season. Nobody has ever been glad they migrated systems in July." — *business*

> "Marcy on the counter knew the pump was failing before the report did. Ask Marcy." — *values*
