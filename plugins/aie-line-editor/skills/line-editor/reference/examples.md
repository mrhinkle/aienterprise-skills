# Worked line-edit examples

These are invented teaching examples, not excerpts from a private manuscript or a report of an automated evaluation.

## Direct edit

Request: “Line edit this paragraph, preserving the narrator's voice.”

Before (42 words, counted by whitespace):

> The key was still in the lock. Mara noticed that the kitchen was completely empty of people. Nobody at all was there. “Well, that's lovely,” she said. She put the bag down on the table and waited for the kettle to boil.

After (30 words, counted by whitespace):

> The key was still in the lock. The kitchen was empty. “Well, that's lovely,” Mara said. She put the bag on the table and waited for the kettle to boil.

Rationale: remove an unnecessary perception filter, duplicate emptiness, and a dispensable particle; retain the dry dialogue and the sequence of events. The cut is 12 words (29 percent), explained by two redundant descriptions of an empty room. The act of noticing becomes implicit, so keep it explicit if that moment matters. The revision introduces no explanation of who left the key or why the kitchen is empty.

Queries: None. Style-sheet additions: None. Verification: compare wording, facts, and voice against the original. No file or repository checks apply to pasted text.

## Guided review

Request: “Walk me through the line edits one at a time.”

Present a single pending card:

### Line edit 1 of about 2 — Redundancy

**Location:** Sentence beginning “Mara noticed…”

**Before**
> Mara noticed that the kitchen was completely empty of people. Nobody at all was there.

**Proposed**
> The kitchen was empty.

**Why:** The second sentence repeats the first, and the perception filter adds distance without new information.

**Effect:** Preserves the empty kitchen; removes the explicit act of noticing. If the moment of recognition matters, keep it.

Reply naturally: **Accept**, **Revise**, **Skip**, **Context**, **Why**, **Undo**, or **Pause**.

At this point the source is unchanged. If the author says “Revise: keep the act of noticing,” offer “Mara noticed that the kitchen was empty” as a revised proposal and wait again. Only acceptance applies that displayed wording. Skipping leaves the original intact; pausing preserves prior accepted edits without applying the pending one.

## Query and leave

- “Alex told Sam that his report was late.” With no clarifying context, ask whose report was late; do not silently assign ownership.
- “Ain't my key,” Mara said. Preserve intentional character speech; do not automatically standardize it to “That isn't my key.”
- “The sensor had missed three readings.” Preserve `three` and the technical claim; do not invent a cause or replacement measurement.

For review-only requests, give these observations without modifying the source. On resume, compare the current target with the prior accepted edits before proposing the next change.
